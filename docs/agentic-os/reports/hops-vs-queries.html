Two ways to reason over a graph: whole queries with the right vocabulary answer 56 / 90, hop actions answer 47 / 90 in half the time and never touch a scan — and both lose the same hard questions

Three questions were open after lab 015: does adding meaning to the schema vocabulary (O1) help beyond the vocabulary itself (S1); does the original Graph-CoT action set — RetrieveNode / NodeFeature / NeighbourCheck / NodeDegree, one hop per call — behave differently from iterative Text2Cypher on the same questions; and what do the questions' own entities tell us about routing. Same 90 GRBench questions (30 per class), same model (Qwen3.6-27B, TP=2, reasoning off), same serving shape (K=8).

lab-s15: H100 PCIe+NVLink pair (UAE), $4.27/h, 75 min, artifacts partly lost, O1 pass salvaged lab-s16: same host, $5.07/h, 78 min, both hop passes complete bundles v43 / v44 · references: B0 lab-s11 k8, S1 lab-s13

Terminology. "Text2Cypher" lanes are the iterative whole-query loop measured in labs 013–015 (GraphCoT-style ReAct, ≤ 6 turns). "Graph-CoT hops" is the new lane with the paper's four actions as fixed Cypher templates behind the same execution boundary (≤ 12 turns, ≤ 4 hops per turn), so the model never writes a query.

2 · What the schema-semantics arm settled

O1 on Cypher ≈ S1 saturated

Relation meanings and directions on top of the S1 vocabulary: hard 7 → 9 answered and exhaustion 22 → 13 (the model stops earlier), but easy 20 → 17 and medium 29 → 24. 50 vs 56 in total, inside what N = 30 cells resolve. The information the model lacked was the vocabulary — which property holds a name, which relation names exist — not what the relations mean.

O1 on SPARQL hurts 4 / 5 / 2 non-empty

With per-relation domain / range classes in the context (≈ 2 500 tokens), GTSQA single still validates 23–26 / 30 but returns rows in only 4 / 5 / 2 cases (B0 10 / 15 / 10). Five context arms later (B0, C-, S1, S4, O1) the SPARQL lane has 0 grounded answers in every cell: its loop, not its context, is the problem.

Cost of the arm

GRBench O1 context 2.8 kB vs S1 0.6 kB; prefill share unchanged at 0.05–0.09; 0 Bolt timeouts in 229 calls; 4 unanchored_scan rejections, all repaired with rows.

Reading for the talk

"Ontology-enriched context" is not a lever on this benchmark once the vocabulary is right. The honest 2×2 is: structure alone (B0) loses, vocabulary (S1) wins, meaning (O1) is neutral-to-negative. The remaining cells (full × ontology) are not worth a session.

  • Search, not anchoring. The paper's design assumes RetrieveNode returns the anchor and the walk starts. This model paraphrases titles and names (lower-casing, dropping words), so four of five RetrieveNode calls only succeed through the substring fallback, and 37–38 cases finish without ever calling NeighbourCheck. In those cases the question was answered from the retrieval hits themselves — a text search over the graph, not a walk.
  • The budget stop is ours. The 16 384-token budget sums every turn's prompt. A hop turn carries 1–2 k tokens of context and the lane takes ~10 turns, so 21–24 cases stop at ≈ 17 k cumulative tokens with rows already in hand — Text2Cypher spends the same budget on 6 turns of repair. A per-case budget on fresh tokens (output + uncached input) would compare the two fairly; this is the first follow-up.
  • No DB risk. Fixed templates cannot scan: 0 timeouts, p90 7–11 ms. Under the K ≥ 16 contention of lab-s12 (Text2Cypher timeouts 3 → 9 per pass) this matters.
  • Where each loses. Medium (counts, "author of both", citation counts): one Cypher does what three hops do — S1 29 vs hops 23. Hard (argmax over a neighbourhood): everyone 5–9; hops hit the budget (11–15 hard cases) or answer without rows (9–13). Easy: tied at 19–20, hops in half the wall.

4 · The questions' own entities (lab 018)

No reuse to exploit

GRBench: 140 questions name 174 entities (60 titles, 76 author names), all distinct, all resolvable to exactly one node by exact title / name; no node is anchored by two questions (cohort: 110 anchor nodes, all distinct). GTSQA: 1 622 cases share 15 % of seed mentions overall (French, German, Berlin, English, World War II…), but the 90-case cohort's 148 seeds are all distinct. Neither cohort has anything to cache by identity.

Hubness sits in the middle class

Anchor out-degree p50: easy 12 (max 132), medium 71 (max 1 823 — one hub author), hard 2 (max 23). Class mix: easy 18 low / 11 mid; medium 8 low / 20 mid / 1 hub; hard 21 low / 1 mid / 8 unresolved. The 43–63 s scans (lab-s7) and the 15 s timeouts (lab-s11/12) came from the medium hubs; hard questions anchor on quiet authors and ask for an argmax over their neighbourhood.

The unresolvable tail is the hard tail

10 cohort questions have no anchor an exact name / title match can find: 6 paper_recommendation (the paper is described, not named) and 4 lower-case names. Both lanes fail these at step 0; they need a retrieval-first path (the CONTAINS fallback is a first approximation).

Routing features available before the LLM runs

Template family (12, regex, 0 unmatched) → anchor type and degree class: authors_of_paper 19, venue_of_paper 9, citation_count 42, author_of_both_papers 75, closest_collaborator 2, most_cited_paper_of_author 1. A router could send lookup families to the hop lane (safe for hubs, half the wall), aggregation families to Text2Cypher S1 (29 / 30), argmax families to Text2Cypher with a finalisation rule, and unresolved anchors to retrieval first; degree class sets row cap / timeout / hop cap. Measured against the best single lane (S1: 56 / 90) that is the next experiment.

5 · Operational notes

  • lab-s15 lost its bulk artifacts: the UAE host uploaded 27 MB in 30 min, the single scp -r hit its 1 800 s cap, and the fail-closed teardown destroyed the instance. The partial download held the complete O1 pass and 84 / 90 hop-B0 cases; graphcot-O1 was rerun in lab-s16.
